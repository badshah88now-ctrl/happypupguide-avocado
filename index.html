Can Dogs Eat Avocado? The Flesh Is Fine in Small Amounts — the Pit Is Not

By HappyPupGuide

Avocado flesh is generally safe for most dogs in small amounts, but the rest of the fruit is not a treat. The flesh is creamy, rich in healthy fats, fibre, potassium and vitamins, and a spoonful can be a nice occasional addition to a meal. The pit, skin and leaves are the parts to keep away from your dog at all times.

The pit is the biggest danger. It is large, hard and slippery, and a dog that swallows it whole can choke or develop a blockage in the gut, which is a veterinary emergency. The skin and leaves contain persin, a compound that can upset a dog's stomach, and the flesh itself is very high in fat, so large portions can trigger stomach upset or, in sensitive dogs, pancreatitis.

If you offer avocado, use only a small amount of plain, ripe flesh — a tablespoon or two for a medium dog, less for a small dog or puppy. Mash it plain, with nothing added: no salt, no garlic, no onion, no chilli and no shop-bought guacamole, which often contains onion, garlic and salt. Never let your dog chew on the pit or the skin.

Watch your dog after the first taste. Most dogs handle a little avocado flesh easily, but a rich, fatty food can loosen stools in a sensitive stomach. If you notice vomiting, diarrhoea or a lack of appetite, stop and give plain food for a day, and speak to your vet if it does not settle. Dogs with a history of pancreatitis or a sensitive stomach should only get avocado after a vet's okay.
